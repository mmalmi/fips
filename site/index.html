Illustrative FIPS network

Many small connections.
One shared network.

Underneath Freedom is FIPS: the Free Internetworking Peering System. Each node knows its neighbors. Together, they find paths across the mesh.

01

Bring a link.

A nearby Wi-Fi peer, an Ethernet cable, or a connection over the existing internet. Different links can be part of the same FIPS network.

02

Own your address.

A node creates its own cryptographic identity. That public key is its address, independent of the IP address or network it happens to use.

03

Pass it along.

Routers carry encrypted traffic between peers. End-to-end encryption protects the contents across the route; each adjacent link is authenticated too.

Share freely.
Or get paid
to forward.

Choose free forwarding or payments by usage. You choose the price of carrying traffic, and the limits on what you spend.

On OpenWrt, a small web interface puts the service, peers and payment credit in Services → Freedom.

Working hardware.
Still for builders.

The scoped relay has been tested on Linux, three OpenWrt routers and an Android test client, including paid forwarding and controlled recovery tests.

This is active development. Public real-money operation, mixed-version upgrades, broad hardware compatibility and seamless mobile roaming are not established. The current setup uses matched development dependencies.

Read the evidence and limits ↗

Before you plug in.

Does this sell ordinary Wi-Fi internet access?

Freedom currently provides free or paid forwarding inside the FIPS network. A captive portal, general internet exit and consumer payment flow are separate integrations. Joining the Wi-Fi alone does not turn an ordinary phone into a paying FIPS client.

Is this tied to Cudy routers?

No. FIPS uses standard interfaces, and Freedom is an optional OpenWrt package. The physical acceptance work used ARM64 Cudy TR3000 v1 hardware. A different router still needs compatible OpenWrt support, enough storage and RAM, and a verified build for its CPU.

Can the routers move around?

Identities do not depend on location. Nearby nodes can discover and connect over a prepared link, and FIPS can find other routes as links change. Radio channels still have to overlap, and seamless roaming or arbitrary movement with uninterrupted payments is not a current guarantee.

Why not just use a normal Wi-Fi mesh?

FIPS routes across different kinds of links and addresses nodes by cryptographic identity. For paid native Wi-Fi forwarding, lower-layer mesh forwarding must be disabled: each paid hop must pass through FIPS rather than bypassing its accounting.

Do I need payments to use FIPS?

No. FIPS routing works without the paid service. Freedom can also offer zero-priced forwarding or destination-specific prices. With paid routes, price and quality selection can compare offers using measured loss and latency; it does not promise a globally cheapest path.
